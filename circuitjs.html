Introduction

Welcome to the Digital Circuit Lab! This platform provides an interactive, real-time environment designed for students, educators, and hobbyists to seamlessly design, visualize, and analyze electronic circuits.

Unlike traditional static circuit diagrams, this simulator brings your designs to life. You can watch the flow of current, observe voltage levels indicated by vivid colors, and view real-time waveforms on the built-in oscilloscope.

Key Features

  • Real-time Simulation: Instantly see the effects of changes to your circuit components without waiting for compilation or static analysis.
  • Comprehensive Component Library: Access a wide array of analog components (resistors, capacitors, inductors), digital logic gates, and specialized ICs.
  • Visual Feedback: Wires dynamically change color to represent positive (green) and negative (red) voltages, while moving dots indicate current flow and direction.
  • Oscilloscope Integration: Plot voltages and currents directly within the browser to analyze signals and time-varying behaviors.

Procedure & Guide

Follow these steps to effectively use the Digital Lab simulator to build and test your circuits.

1. Adding Components

Use the Draw menu at the top of the screen (or simply right-click anywhere on the blank canvas) to select a component. Click and drag your mouse across the canvas to place and size the component.

2. Wiring the Circuit

Select Add Wire (Shortcut: w). Click and drag from the terminal of one component to the terminal of another. Ensure the nodes properly snap together. You must always provide a Ground connection for the simulation to reference 0V.

3. Running the Simulation

Once your circuit is wired, click the prominent green ▶ RUN button at the top left. The button will turn red and read ⏹ STOP, indicating the simulation is active. You will see dots moving along the wires if current is flowing.

4. Editing Values

To change the resistance, capacitance, or voltage of an element, simply double-click the component. A dialog box will appear allowing you to input exact values.

5. Using the Oscilloscope

Hover your mouse over a component, right-click, and select View in Scope. A waveform graph will appear at the bottom of the screen, tracking the voltage and current of that component in real-time.
